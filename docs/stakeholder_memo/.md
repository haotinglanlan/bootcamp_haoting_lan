# Stakeholder Memo: Consumer Loan Default Risk Prediction

**To:** Credit Risk Manager
**From:** Project Team
**Subject:** Using Default Risk Prediction to Support Credit Review

## Problem Context

For consumer lenders, one of the most important risks comes from borrowers who are unable to repay their loans. Although lenders can review borrowers individually before making a lending decision, conducting a detailed manual review for every applicant can require substantial time and resources. At the same time, failing to identify borrowers with relatively high default risk can lead to financial losses for the lender. Therefore, it would be useful to develop a more systematic way to identify which borrowers may require additional attention during the credit review process.

This project focuses on whether historical borrower and loan characteristics can be used to predict the probability that a borrower will default. Rather than attempting to completely replace the existing credit review process, the purpose of the model is to provide additional information that can help the credit risk team identify potentially higher-risk borrowers and allocate their review resources more efficiently.

## Stakeholder and End User

The primary stakeholder for this project is the **Credit Risk Manager**, whose responsibility is to manage the lender’s credit exposure and reduce potential losses associated with borrower defaults. Credit analysts and loan officers would serve as the main end users because they are more directly involved in reviewing borrower information and making or supporting lending decisions.

In practice, the model would be used before or during the credit review process. It would provide an estimated default probability for each borrower, allowing analysts to compare borrowers based on their relative level of risk. In particular, borrowers with relatively high predicted default probabilities could receive additional review before a final lending decision is made.

## Useful Answer and Decision

The project is primarily **predictive rather than causal**. The goal is not to determine whether a particular borrower characteristic directly causes default. Instead, the analysis will examine whether the information available about a borrower at the time of the lending decision can provide a meaningful prediction of future default risk.

The main output of the project will be an estimated probability of default for each borrower. These probabilities can then be used to rank borrowers by risk or to identify borrowers whose predicted risk exceeds a selected threshold. Model performance can be evaluated using measures such as ROC-AUC, recall, and precision. In particular, recall will be useful for understanding how many borrowers who eventually default are successfully identified by the model, while precision can help show how often borrowers classified as high risk actually default.

The purpose of these predictions is not to automatically approve or reject borrowers. Rather, they can provide an additional signal that helps the credit risk team decide which cases may require more careful review.

## Assumptions and Constraints

The analysis assumes that historical borrower and loan information is available and that the dataset contains a reasonably clear definition of whether a borrower eventually defaulted. It is also important that the variables used for prediction represent information that would realistically have been available before the lending decision. Otherwise, the model could appear to perform well historically while providing limited value in an actual lending setting.

There are also several possible data constraints. For example, some borrower characteristics may contain missing observations, and the number of default cases may be substantially smaller than the number of non-default cases. In addition, historical borrowers may not perfectly represent future applicants. As a result, strong model performance within the available dataset does not necessarily guarantee that the same level of performance will continue in a different lending environment.

## Success Criteria

A useful model should be able to distinguish relatively higher-risk borrowers from lower-risk borrowers and identify a meaningful share of borrowers who eventually default. However, identifying as many potential defaults as possible is not the only objective. If the model incorrectly flags too many low-risk borrowers, the lender may have to conduct unnecessary additional reviews, which would reduce the efficiency gains provided by the model.

Therefore, the final evaluation should consider the tradeoff between identifying more actual defaults and limiting unnecessary high-risk classifications. In this sense, the value of the model depends not only on its overall predictive accuracy, but also on whether its predictions can be incorporated into the lender’s existing credit review process in a practical and interpretable way.
